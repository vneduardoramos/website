## The question behind the question

When a company asks us how to choose a Snowflake partner, the real question is usually different. It is not "who is certified?" It is "who will still be accountable when this is running in production and the business is depending on it?"

Certifications and partner tiers matter, but they are the floor, not the answer. After years of being brought in to finish work another firm started, we have learned that the traits separating a good Snowflake partner from a painful one are rarely on the pitch deck. Here is what we would look for if we were on your side of the table.

## Start with the partner tier, then look past it

Snowflake publishes its partner tiers for a reason: they are earned through certified people and delivered work, not bought. Confirm the basics first.

- Are they a **Snowflake Premier Partner** (or higher), and can they show it on Snowflake's own [partner directory](https://www.snowflake.com/en/why-snowflake/partners/all-partners/)?
- Are the engineers who will actually do your work **SnowPro-certified**, or just a few named leads?
- Do they have direct access to Snowflake's product teams when a build hits a hard edge?

The tier tells you they have cleared a bar. It does not tell you whether the senior people you met in the sales meeting are the ones who will be in your Slack channel six weeks later. Ask that directly.

## Do they build with your team, or hand you a black box?

This is the single biggest predictor of whether you will be happy in a year. Some firms build a solution *for* you and leave. When something breaks or the business changes, you call them back, and the meter starts again.

The alternative is a partner who builds *with* your team: your people in the room, decisions documented, the work handed over so your team can run and extend it without them. That is the model we hold to, and it is worth asking any partner to describe theirs concretely. What does handover actually include? Documentation, enablement, a transition plan? Or a wiki page and a goodbye?

## Match the delivery model to how you work

The best architecture in the world is slow to build if the team delivering it is asleep while yours is awake. For US companies, a partner working in your time zone changes the pace of the whole engagement: same-day answers, live pairing, standups that actually happen. This is where a [nearshore Snowflake team](/nearshore) earns its keep over a purely offshore one, and it is worth pushing on: where are the people, what hours do they keep, and how much of the work is in-time-zone versus handed off overnight?

## Understand how they price, and what that rewards

Pricing is not just a number; it tells you what the partner is optimizing for. A firm that bills by the hour is, structurally, rewarded for the work taking longer. A firm that prices on the outcome is rewarded for finishing. Neither is automatically right, but you should know which one you are buying, and whether the incentive points the same direction as yours.

Ask how a first engagement is scoped. A serious partner will propose a discovery that fixes scope before the full build, and use-case sprints that reach production in weeks with proof before you commit to scale, not a fixed bid on a spec nobody fully understands yet.

## Ask for proof, not adjectives

Anyone can say they are experienced. Ask for the specifics:

- Two or three references you can actually call, ideally in your industry or with your data challenges.
- A [migration](/migrations) or build they can walk you through end to end: what broke, how they handled it, what the client runs today.
- How they handle governance, security, and cost from day one, not as an afterthought (row-level security, masking, resource monitors, consumption visibility).

## A short checklist to take into the meeting

- Are they Premier tier, and are the assigned engineers SnowPro-certified?
- Will the senior people in the pitch be on the delivery team?
- Do they build with your team and hand the work over, or build and leave?
- What are the delivery hours, and how much overlaps yours?
- Does the pricing reward finishing, or reward billing more hours?
- How is the first engagement scoped: discovery and sprints, or a fixed bid on a fuzzy spec?
- Can they give references and a real project they can walk you through?
- Is governance and cost control designed in from the start?

## The honest version

There is no perfect partner, only the right fit for how your team works and what you are trying to ship. The firms worth hiring will answer these questions plainly, show you real work, and stay accountable after the invoice clears. The partner who welcomes the hard questions is usually the one worth choosing.
